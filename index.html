    Cardiovascular and Cerebrovascular

    16 parameters
    Blood Viscosity(N)
    This measures how thick and syrupy your blood is. Blood needs to flow smoothly through your vessels — if it becomes too thick, it moves more slowly and puts extra strain on your heart. People with high blood viscosity are at greater risk of strokes, heart attacks, and blood clots. Dehydration, high cholesterol, and certain conditions can all increase blood viscosity.
    ▲If this reading is highDrink more water through the day and go easy on fatty, fried foods, since dehydration and high cholesterol are common drivers of thick blood. Regular gentle movement like walking also helps blood flow more smoothly.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow blood viscosity simply means your blood is flowing easily and isn't sluggish, which is good for your heart, not a problem to fix. Just keep drinking enough water and eating well to stay in this comfortable range.
    Hyperviscosity state
    When blood viscosity is too high, blood flows sluggishly through the vessels. For people with high blood pressure or heart disease, this significantly raises the risk of a stroke or heart attack. Think of it like trying to pump honey through a pipe instead of water — the heart has to work much harder.
    ▲If this reading is highFocus on hydration, lighter meals, and daily movement to help blood flow more easily, and mention it to a doctor if you also have high blood pressure or heart disease since the combination raises stroke risk.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading here means your blood is moving smoothly rather than sluggishly, which takes strain off the heart, so there's nothing to worry about. Keep up the hydration and activity that got you here.
    Cholesterol Crystal
    Cholesterol crystals form when there is too much cholesterol in the blood and it starts to clump. High levels are associated with early-stage artery hardening (atherosclerosis) and chest pain. Low levels may indicate poor nutrition or a weakened immune system. Both extremes are worth monitoring.
    ▲If this reading is highCut back on saturated fat and sugar, add more fibre, and stay active to help bring cholesterol clumping down.
    ▼If this reading is lowMake sure you are eating enough overall and getting balanced nutrition, since very low levels can sometimes point to poor nutrition or a weakened immune system.
    Blood Fat
    Blood fats (lipids) include cholesterol and triglycerides. When these are too high, fatty deposits build up inside artery walls, narrowing them over time. This is a major risk factor for heart disease and stroke. High blood fats can be caused by genetics, an unhealthy diet, diabetes, thyroid problems, or kidney disease.
    ▲If this reading is highReduce fried and processed foods, add more vegetables and fibre, and keep up regular exercise, this is one of the most diet-responsive numbers on the whole scan.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow blood fat means your cholesterol and triglycerides aren't building up in your artery walls, which is exactly what you want, not a concern. Keep eating well and staying active to maintain it.
    Vascular Resistance
    This measures how hard your heart has to push to move blood through your blood vessels. Higher resistance means narrower or stiffer vessels — a sign of high blood pressure or arterial disease. Lower resistance means blood flows easily. When resistance is too low, it can indicate abnormally low blood pressure.
    ▲If this reading is highCut back on salt, manage stress, and stay active to help your vessels relax and ease the load on your heart.
    ▼If this reading is lowStand up slowly and stay well hydrated, since unusually low resistance can go along with low blood pressure and lightheadedness.
    Vascular Elasticity
    Healthy arteries are flexible — they expand when the heart beats and spring back between beats. This elasticity acts as a natural buffer, smoothing out blood flow. Stiff, inelastic arteries (a sign of aging or arterial disease) mean less buffering, higher peak blood pressure, and increased strain on the heart.
    ▲If this reading is highHigh vascular elasticity means your arteries are nicely flexible and springing back well with each heartbeat, which is a great sign, not a problem. Keep up the aerobic exercise and low-salt habits that support this.
    ▼If this reading is lowRegular aerobic exercise, less salt, and not smoking all help keep artery walls more flexible over time.
    Myocardial Blood Demand
    This is the amount of blood the heart muscle needs to receive every minute to function properly. Like any working muscle, the heart demands more blood when it is working harder. If the coronary arteries cannot meet this demand, it can cause chest pain (angina) or a heart attack.
    ▲If this reading is highPace yourself with exertion and build up activity gradually, a heart working harder than usual benefits from steady, moderate rather than sudden intense effort.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading here just means your heart isn't working especially hard right now, which is a sign of an easy-going workload rather than a problem. Regular gentle activity will keep your heart efficiently conditioned.
    Myocardial Blood Perfusion Volume
    This is the actual amount of blood being delivered to the heart muscle per minute. If this falls below what the heart needs (the blood demand), the heart muscle becomes starved of oxygen — a condition that can cause chest pain, damage the heart muscle, or trigger a heart attack.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your heart muscle is getting plenty of blood and oxygen, which is exactly what you want, not a cause for concern. Keep up the activity and good circulation habits that support this.
    ▼If this reading is lowTake it easy on strenuous activity and let a doctor know if you notice chest discomfort, since the heart muscle may not be getting all the blood it needs.
    Myocardial Oxygen Consumption
    This is the amount of oxygen the heart muscle uses per minute. The heart's oxygen use goes up when your heart beats faster, contracts more forcefully, or beats for longer. Ideally, the heart should do maximum useful work while consuming as little oxygen as possible — this is the sign of an efficient, healthy heart.
    ▲If this reading is highBuild cardio fitness gradually and manage stress and blood pressure, an efficient heart does more work for less oxygen, and steady exercise helps train that efficiency.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading here is actually the sign of an efficient heart doing its job using less oxygen, not a problem at all. Keep building cardio fitness gradually to maintain that efficiency.
    Stroke Volume
    The amount of blood your heart pumps out with each single beat. A healthy resting adult pumps around 60–100 mL per beat. Low stroke volume means the heart is not filling or contracting efficiently. This can be caused by blood loss, dehydration, heart muscle weakness, or stiff arteries making it harder to eject blood.
    ▲If this reading is highA high stroke volume means your heart is filling and contracting strongly, pumping a healthy amount of blood with each beat, which is a good sign rather than a worry. Staying active and well hydrated helps keep it that way.
    ▼If this reading is lowStay well hydrated and avoid standing for long periods without moving, mild dehydration or fluid loss can lower how much blood the heart pumps per beat.
    Left Ventricular Ejection Impedance
    This measures the resistance the left ventricle (the main pumping chamber) faces when it tries to push blood out into the aorta. High impedance — caused by things like a narrowed aortic valve or high blood pressure — means the heart must squeeze much harder to eject blood, which strains and thickens the heart muscle over time.
    ▲If this reading is highKeep blood pressure in check through diet, salt reduction, and activity, since high resistance here makes the heart work harder with every beat.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your heart isn't fighting much resistance to push blood out, so it's working easily rather than straining, which is good news. Keeping blood pressure healthy through diet and activity will help maintain it.
    Left Ventricular Effective Pump Power
    This reflects how powerfully the left ventricle contracts to push blood around the body. The normal resting value is about 1.8 kg of force. Low pump power means the heart is contracting weakly — the heart muscle fibres may be damaged or diseased. High pump power means the heart is contracting strongly and ejecting a good volume of blood.
    ▲If this reading is highHigh pump power means your heart is contracting strongly and ejecting a good volume of blood, which is a sign of a powerful, healthy heart rather than a concern. Regular cardio exercise helps keep that strength up.
    ▼If this reading is lowEase into exercise rather than pushing hard, and rest when tired, weak pump power means giving your heart muscle time and gentle conditioning rather than sudden strain.
    Coronary Artery Elasticity
    The coronary arteries are the three blood vessels that supply the heart muscle itself with blood. Just like other arteries, they need to remain flexible and open to deliver adequate blood flow. When they stiffen or narrow — due to plaque buildup from high cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, or high blood pressure — the heart muscle is at risk of oxygen starvation.
    ▲If this reading is highHigh elasticity here means the vessels feeding your own heart muscle are flexible and open, which is exactly what you want, not a worry. Keep cholesterol in check and stay active to protect that flexibility.
    ▼If this reading is lowReduce cholesterol-heavy foods, stop smoking if you smoke, and stay active, these all help keep the vessels feeding your heart muscle open and flexible.
    Coronary Perfusion Pressure
    This is the blood pressure that drives blood through the coronary arteries to nourish the heart muscle. It is mainly determined by the blood pressure during the relaxation phase of the heartbeat (diastolic pressure). When coronary perfusion pressure is too low, parts of the heart muscle do not receive enough blood, which can progress to a heart attack.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your heart muscle is getting strong, steady blood flow through its own arteries, which is a good thing rather than a concern. Keeping blood pressure and activity levels steady helps maintain it.
    ▼If this reading is lowAvoid sudden drops in activity intensity and stay hydrated, and flag persistent low readings to a doctor since the heart muscle relies on this pressure for its own blood supply.
    Cerebral Blood Vessel Elasticity
    This reflects the flexibility and health of the blood vessels supplying the brain. When brain arteries become stiff and narrow — due to atherosclerosis (hardening of the arteries) — blood flow to the brain is reduced. This raises the risk of blood clots forming in the brain (cerebral thrombosis) or blood vessels bursting (haemorrhagic stroke).
    ▲If this reading is highHigh elasticity here means the blood vessels feeding your brain are flexible and open, supporting good blood flow, which is a positive sign, not a concern. Keep up the blood pressure and cholesterol habits that support this.
    ▼If this reading is lowKeep blood pressure and cholesterol in a healthy range and stay physically active, these support flexible, open blood vessels feeding the brain.
    Brain Tissue Blood Supply Status
    This measures how well blood is reaching the brain tissue. The brain depends entirely on continuous blood flow for oxygen and nutrients. When blood supply is disrupted — either because a vessel is blocked (ischaemic) or because a vessel bursts (haemorrhagic) — brain cells begin to die within minutes, causing a stroke.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your brain is getting plenty of oxygen-rich blood, which is great news rather than something to worry about. Staying hydrated and active helps keep that steady flow going.
    ▼If this reading is lowStay hydrated, keep moving through the day, and manage blood pressure, good circulation habits support steady blood flow to the brain.

    Gastrointestinal Function

    5 parameters
    Pepsin Secretion Coefficient
    This measures how well your stomach is producing pepsin, the enzyme that breaks down proteins from your food. The stomach also produces hydrochloric acid and protective mucus. Together, these help digest food and kill harmful bacteria. Too little pepsin means protein digestion is impaired; too much can contribute to stomach ulcers if the protective mucus lining is weakened.
    ▲If this reading is highEat smaller, calmer meals and avoid foods that aggravate the stomach lining like very spicy or acidic items, since excess pepsin alongside weak mucus protection can contribute to ulcers.
    ▼If this reading is lowChew food thoroughly and consider smaller, protein-light meals, low pepsin can make protein harder to break down.
    Gastric Peristalsis Function Coefficient
    Your stomach walls are made of layers of muscle that squeeze and churn your food — a movement called peristalsis. This mixing action combines food with digestive juices to break it down into a smooth paste (chyme) that can pass into the small intestine. Poor peristalsis means food sits in the stomach too long, causing bloating, discomfort, and poor digestion.
    ▲If this reading is highA low reading here means your stomach is churning and mixing food efficiently rather than too sluggishly, which is good digestion at work, not a problem. Keep eating regular, relaxed meals to support it.
    ▼If this reading is lowEat smaller meals, chew well, and take a short walk after eating, this helps food move along and eases bloating and discomfort from sluggish stomach movement.
    Gastric Absorption Function Coefficient
    While the stomach is mainly a digestion organ, its lining does absorb some substances — primarily water, alcohol, and certain medications. An adult stomach produces 1.5–2.5 litres of gastric juice per day. This index measures how effectively the stomach's lining is functioning in its absorption and protective roles.
    ▲If this reading is highA low reading just means your stomach lining is functioning comfortably within its normal absorptive and protective role, nothing to be concerned about. Staying hydrated and easy on alcohol keeps it working well.
    ▼If this reading is lowStay hydrated and avoid excess alcohol, which can further strain the stomach lining's absorptive and protective roles.
    Small Intestine Peristalsis Function Coefficient
    The small intestine moves food along using rhythmic contractions (peristalsis). This movement mixes food with digestive enzymes, pushes it against the intestinal wall for absorption, and propels it towards the large intestine. Slow peristalsis leads to bloating, constipation, and poor nutrient absorption; too fast means nutrients are not fully absorbed.
    ▲If this reading is highIf things are moving too fast, simple, easy-to-digest meals and good hydration can help nutrients get properly absorbed before passing through.
    ▼If this reading is lowAdd more fibre, water, and light movement to your day to help keep things moving and prevent bloating or constipation.
    Small Intestine Absorption Function Coefficient
    This is where almost all your nutrients are absorbed. The small intestine absorbs sugars (broken into simple glucose), amino acids (from proteins), and fats (broken into fatty acids). It also absorbs vitamins, minerals, and water. Poor absorption here leads to nutritional deficiencies, weight loss, and digestive symptoms even if you are eating enough.
    ▲If this reading is highA low reading here means your small intestine is absorbing nutrients efficiently rather than struggling, which is exactly what you want. Keep eating a varied, nutrient-rich diet to support that good absorption.
    ▼If this reading is lowFocus on nutrient-dense, easy-to-digest meals and consider discussing persistent symptoms with a doctor, since poor absorption here can cause deficiencies even with a good diet.

    Large Intestine Function

    4 parameters
    Large intestine peristalsis function coefficient
    The large intestine (colon) moves waste material along slowly — much more slowly than the small intestine — giving it time to absorb water and electrolytes. If the colon moves too slowly, too much water is removed from waste, causing hard stools and constipation. If it moves too fast, not enough water is absorbed, causing loose stools and diarrhoea.
    ▲If this reading is highIf things are moving too fast, simple bland meals and good hydration can help your colon absorb enough water and ease loose stools.
    ▼If this reading is lowAdd more fibre, fluids, and daily movement to help keep your colon moving and prevent hard stools and constipation.
    Colonic absorption coefficient
    The colon's primary job is to absorb water and minerals (electrolytes) from waste material, converting liquid waste into solid faeces. It can absorb up to 1.5 litres of water per day. Good colonic absorption helps maintain the body's fluid and electrolyte balance. Poor absorption leads to dehydration or electrolyte imbalances.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your colon is doing a great job pulling water and minerals back into your body, keeping your fluid and electrolyte balance steady, which is a good thing. Keep drinking enough water to support that process.
    ▼If this reading is lowDrink plenty of water and include electrolyte-rich foods, poor colonic absorption can throw off your fluid and mineral balance.
    Intestinal bacteria coefficient
    A healthy gut contains billions of beneficial bacteria that create a slightly acidic environment, crowd out harmful bacteria, aid digestion, and support the immune system. This index measures the balance between good and bad bacteria in your gut (the microbiome). An imbalance — caused by antibiotics, poor diet, or illness — is linked to digestive problems, weakened immunity, and a range of other health issues.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading here means your gut bacteria are in a healthy, beneficial balance, which is good news for digestion and immunity, not a concern. Keep enjoying fibre and fermented foods to keep that balance thriving.
    ▼If this reading is lowAdd fermented foods, fibre, and a varied diet to support a healthier balance of gut bacteria, especially if you have recently taken antibiotics.
    Intraluminal pressure coefficient
    This measures the gas pressure inside the intestine. Some gas is normal — it is produced by bacteria fermenting food. Excessive gas causes bloating, cramps, and discomfort. Too much gas pressure can result from slow intestinal movement, food intolerances, swallowing air, or an imbalance in gut bacteria.
    ▲If this reading is highNotice if certain foods trigger more gas and bloating, eat a bit more slowly to avoid swallowing air, and keep meals regular to support steady gut movement.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your gut isn't building up excess gas pressure, so digestion is moving along comfortably, which is a good sign. Keep your regular meal habits and gut-friendly foods going to maintain it.

    Liver Function

    5 parameters
    Protein Metabolism
    The liver is your body's protein factory. It takes amino acids absorbed from digested food and builds them into hundreds of different proteins your body needs — including blood proteins like albumin, clotting factors, and immune proteins. It also breaks down excess or damaged proteins. Poor liver protein metabolism leads to fluid retention, clotting problems, and weakened immunity.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your liver is efficiently producing the blood proteins, clotting factors, and immune proteins your body needs, which is a good sign, not a worry. Keep eating enough quality protein to keep supporting it.
    ▼If this reading is lowMake sure you're getting enough quality protein and rest, since the liver needs support to keep producing the proteins your blood and immune system rely on.
    Energy Production Function
    The liver is the body's central energy processing plant. It converts digested carbohydrates into glucose for immediate energy use, stores excess glucose as glycogen for later, and converts fat into energy when needed. When you haven't eaten for a while, the liver releases stored glucose to keep your blood sugar stable.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your liver is doing a great job converting food into usable energy and keeping your blood sugar steady, which is exactly what you want. Regular balanced meals help keep this running smoothly.
    ▼If this reading is lowEat regular balanced meals rather than skipping them, this helps the liver keep your blood sugar steady between meals.
    Detoxification Function
    The liver neutralises harmful substances — including alcohol, drugs, metabolic waste, and environmental toxins — and converts them into harmless forms that can be excreted in urine or bile. This is one of the liver's most critical functions. A damaged liver cannot detoxify properly, allowing harmful substances to build up in the body.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your liver is clearing out toxins and waste products efficiently, which is a sign of a healthy liver, not a problem. Keep going easy on alcohol and unnecessary medications to help it keep up the good work.
    ▼If this reading is lowCut back on alcohol and unnecessary medications where possible, and give your liver lighter work so it can keep clearing out everyday toxins effectively.
    Bile Secretion Function
    The liver produces bile, a digestive fluid that is stored in the gallbladder and released into the small intestine after meals. Bile breaks down dietary fats so they can be absorbed. It also carries fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K) into the body. Without adequate bile, fat digestion and absorption of these essential vitamins are impaired.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your liver is producing plenty of bile to break down fats and carry in fat-soluble vitamins, which is good digestive function, not a concern. Keep enjoying a balanced diet with healthy fats to support it.
    ▼If this reading is lowGo easy on very fatty meals and consider whether you're getting enough fat-soluble vitamins, since low bile production makes fat digestion harder.
    Liver Fat Content
    The liver normally contains a small amount of fat. A fatty liver (hepatic steatosis) is diagnosed when fat makes up more than 5% of the liver's weight. This is commonly caused by excessive alcohol consumption, obesity, or diabetes (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease). If untreated, a fatty liver can progress to inflammation, scarring (cirrhosis), and liver failure.
    ▲If this reading is highCut back on alcohol, added sugar, and fried foods, and add regular movement, fatty liver often improves well with diet and exercise changes alone.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your liver is carrying little to no extra fat, which is exactly the healthy state you want, not a concern. Keep up the balanced diet and regular movement that's keeping it that way.

    Gallbladder Function

    5 parameters
    Serum Globulin (A/G)
    This measures the ratio of albumin to globulin proteins in your blood. Albumin helps carry nutrients and maintain fluid balance, while globulins are your immune proteins. A healthy ratio means your liver is producing the right mix of proteins. A low ratio can indicate liver disease, kidney problems, or immune system issues.
    ▲If this reading is highA high ratio means your liver is producing a healthy mix of albumin and immune proteins, which is a good sign rather than a worry. Keep eating a balanced diet with enough protein to support it.
    ▼If this reading is lowMake sure your diet includes enough protein and mention a persistently low ratio to a doctor, since it can reflect liver, kidney, or immune changes.
    Total Bilirubin (TBIL)
    Bilirubin is a yellow pigment made when your body breaks down old red blood cells. The liver processes it and sends it into bile. High bilirubin causes jaundice (yellowing of skin and eyes) and may signal liver problems, gallstones blocking the bile duct, or excessive red blood cell breakdown.
    ▲If this reading is highWatch for yellowing of the skin or eyes and mention it to a doctor, high bilirubin is worth checking out rather than waiting on, since it can point to a bile flow or liver issue.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your liver is clearing old red blood cell byproducts efficiently and bile is flowing well, which is good news, not a concern. There's nothing extra you need to do here, just keep up your usual healthy habits.
    Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)
    An enzyme found mainly in your liver, bones, and bile ducts. High levels often indicate a bile duct blockage (such as from gallstones), liver disease, or rapid bone growth. It is routinely checked to assess liver and gallbladder health.
    ▲If this reading is highIf you're an adult and not in a growth phase, mention a high reading to a doctor, since it often relates to bile flow or liver health worth a closer look.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading is generally a sign that your liver, bile ducts, and bones are working normally without strain, not something to worry about. No special action needed beyond your usual healthy routine.
    Serum Total Bile Acid (TBA)
    Bile acids are made in the liver and stored in the gallbladder to help digest fats. Measuring total bile acids in the blood is one of the most sensitive ways to detect early liver disease. Elevated levels suggest the liver is not processing bile correctly.
    ▲If this reading is highTreat this as an early flag worth mentioning to a doctor, since it's one of the most sensitive signs of liver strain even before other symptoms appear.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your liver is processing bile acids properly with no early signs of strain, which is reassuring rather than concerning. Just keep up your regular healthy habits to maintain it.
    Bilirubin (DBIL)
    Direct bilirubin is the processed form that the liver has made water-soluble. High levels indicate the liver has processed bilirubin, but it cannot flow properly into the intestines — often caused by bile duct blockage, gallstones, or liver cell damage.
    ▲If this reading is highMention a raised reading to a doctor, since it usually points to something slowing bile's path out of the liver, like gallstones or a blocked duct.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means bilirubin is flowing out of your liver and into your intestines as it should, with no blockage, which is a good sign. There's nothing to change here, just keep up your usual liver-friendly habits.

    Pancreatic Function

    3 parameters
    Insulin
    Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that acts like a key, unlocking your body's cells to let glucose (sugar) in from the bloodstream for energy. Without enough insulin — or if your cells stop responding to it — blood sugar rises too high, damaging blood vessels and organs over time. This is the mechanism behind diabetes.
    ▲If this reading is highCut back on refined carbs and sugary drinks and stay active, both help your cells respond better to insulin and ease the load on your pancreas.
    ▼If this reading is lowKeep meals regular and balanced, and mention persistently low readings to a doctor since the body needs adequate insulin to manage blood sugar.
    Pancreatic Polypeptide
    Pancreatic polypeptide is a hormone released by the pancreas mainly in response to eating. It helps regulate digestion by slowing down the release of digestive enzymes and the movement of the gut. It also plays a role in appetite regulation and energy balance, though its exact functions in humans are still being studied.
    ▲If this reading is highNo specific action usually needed, just keep meals regular since this hormone responds naturally to eating patterns.
    ▼If this reading is lowEating regular, balanced meals supports normal digestive hormone signalling.
    Glucagon
    Glucagon is the hormone that does the opposite of insulin. When blood sugar drops too low, the pancreas releases glucagon to signal the liver to release stored glucose back into the bloodstream. Insulin and glucagon work as a pair to keep blood sugar within a healthy range — like a thermostat for blood glucose levels.
    ▲If this reading is highKeep an eye on blood sugar patterns and avoid long gaps without eating, since this can be a sign your body is compensating for low glucose availability.
    ▼If this reading is lowEat regularly to avoid long fasting stretches, which can strain the glucagon response that keeps blood sugar from dropping too low.

    Kidney Function

    4 parameters
    Urobilinogen Index
    Urobilinogen is a colourless chemical formed in the intestines when bacteria break down bilirubin (a waste product from old red blood cells). Some is reabsorbed into the blood and excreted by the kidneys into urine. Elevated urobilinogen in urine can indicate liver disease or increased red blood cell breakdown. Very low levels may indicate bile duct obstruction.
    ▲If this reading is highMention raised readings to a doctor, since they can reflect liver strain or faster red blood cell turnover worth investigating.
    ▼If this reading is lowVery low readings can suggest a blocked bile duct, worth flagging to a doctor especially alongside pale stools or dark urine.
    Uric acid Index
    Uric acid is a waste product formed when the body breaks down purines (found in meat, organ meats, and some seafood). Normally it dissolves in the blood and is excreted by the kidneys. High uric acid levels cause it to crystallise in joints, leading to the intensely painful condition called gout. It can also form kidney stones.
    ▲If this reading is highCut back on red meat, organ meats, shellfish, and alcohol, and drink plenty of water, these steps help lower the risk of gout flares and kidney stones.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your body is clearing uric acid well, with little risk of it crystallising into painful gout or kidney stones, which is good news. Keep enjoying a balanced diet without overdoing purine-rich foods like organ meats and shellfish.
    Blood urea nitrogen(BUN) Index
    Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) measures a waste product from protein breakdown. The liver converts the by-products of protein digestion into urea, which the kidneys then filter out of the blood into urine. High BUN suggests the kidneys are not filtering efficiently — possibly due to kidney disease, dehydration, or high protein intake. Low BUN may indicate liver problems or malnutrition.
    ▲If this reading is highStay well hydrated and moderate very high protein intake, dehydration and excess protein are common everyday causes of a raised reading.
    ▼If this reading is lowMake sure you're eating enough protein, low BUN can sometimes reflect undernutrition.
    Proteinuria Index
    Healthy kidneys filter waste while keeping proteins in the blood. If protein appears in the urine in significant amounts (proteinuria), it means the kidney filters are damaged and leaking. Proteinuria is an early warning sign of kidney disease, diabetes-related kidney damage, or high blood pressure affecting the kidneys.
    ▲If this reading is highMention protein in urine to a doctor, especially if you have diabetes or high blood pressure, since it's an early sign worth catching before kidney function declines further.
    ▼If this reading is lowA low reading means your kidney filters are holding onto protein the way they should, with no leakage, which is a good sign of healthy kidney function. Keep up routine checkups, especially if you have diabetes or high blood pressure, just to stay ahead of things.

    Lung Function

    5 parameters
    Vital Capacity (VC)
    The maximum amount of air you can breathe out after taking the deepest breath possible. It reflects the overall size and strength of your lungs. Lower values may indicate asthma, COPD, or weakened breathing muscles.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your lungs and breathing muscles can move a large amount of air, which is a sign of strong respiratory health, not a concern. Staying active and practicing good breathing habits keeps that capacity up.
    ▼If this reading is lowPractice deep breathing exercises and stay active, building lung and breathing-muscle strength can help improve how much air you can move.
    Total Lung Capacity (TLC)
    The total volume of air your lungs can hold when fully inflated. It helps doctors understand whether your lungs are restricted (too small) or overinflated (as in emphysema). Normal values depend on your age, height, and sex.
    ▲If this reading is highAn overinflated reading is worth discussing with a doctor, particularly if you have a smoking history or breathlessness.
    ▼If this reading is lowBreathing exercises and staying active can help if your lungs feel more restricted than they should be, and it's worth mentioning to a doctor if breathlessness is present.
    Airway Resistance (RAM)
    How easily air flows through your airways. Higher resistance means your airways are narrower or partially blocked — as seen in asthma or bronchitis — making breathing harder. Lower resistance means air moves freely.
    ▲If this reading is highAvoid smoke and known triggers, and try slow, controlled breathing, narrowed airways benefit from reducing irritants and staying calm during flare-ups.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow resistance means air is moving freely through your airways without obstruction, which is exactly what you want, not a problem. Keep avoiding smoke and irritants to keep your airways this clear.
    Arterial Oxygen Content (PaO2)
    How well your lungs are transferring oxygen into your bloodstream. Low oxygen levels (hypoxemia) cause shortness of breath, confusion, and organ damage if untreated.
    ▲If this reading is highA high reading means your lungs are transferring oxygen into your blood very effectively, which is a great sign rather than a concern. Keep up regular activity and avoiding smoke exposure to support healthy lung function.
    ▼If this reading is lowRest, avoid smoke exposure, and seek medical attention if you feel short of breath or confused, low oxygen levels need prompt attention rather than waiting it out.
    Arterial Carbon Dioxide (PaCO2)
    The pressure of carbon dioxide in your arterial blood — the waste gas you exhale. High levels mean you are not breathing enough to remove CO2. Low levels may mean you are breathing too fast (hyperventilation).

Exposure can come from eating contaminated fish (especially large predatory fish like tuna and swordfish), dental amalgam fillings, and industrial pollution.     ▼If this reading is lowLow progesterone is linked to irregular cycles and PMS symptoms, so stress reduction and steady sleep support hormone balance, and a doctor's input helps if trying to conceive.
    Luteinizing Hormone (LH)
    Luteinizing hormone (LH) is produced by the pituitary gland and plays a central role in the female reproductive cycle. LH works alongside FSH to stimulate follicle development and oestrogen production. Most importantly, a sudden surge in LH mid-cycle (the LH surge) triggers ovulation — the release of a mature egg from the ovary. After ovulation, LH stimulates the empty follicle to transform into the corpus luteum, which produces progesterone to support a potential pregnancy. Home ovulation predictor kits detect this LH surge. Abnormal LH levels can disrupt ovulation, causing irregular periods and fertility difficulties.
    ▲If this reading is highPersistently high LH can disrupt ovulation and is worth discussing with a doctor if cycles are irregular.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow LH can prevent the ovulation surge from happening properly, which is worth raising with a doctor if you are tracking fertility.
    Prolactin (PRL)
    Prolactin is a hormone produced by the pituitary gland, best known for stimulating breast milk production after childbirth. During late pregnancy and breastfeeding, prolactin levels are naturally high. Outside of pregnancy and breastfeeding, persistently high prolactin suppresses the hormones that trigger ovulation — leading to irregular or absent periods, reduced libido, unexplained milky nipple discharge (galactorrhoea), and difficulty conceiving. Elevated prolactin in non-pregnant women is most commonly caused by a small benign pituitary tumour called a prolactinoma, certain medications, or chronic stress. It is a treatable and important cause of female infertility.
    ▲If this reading is highHigh prolactin outside of pregnancy or breastfeeding can disrupt cycles and fertility, so managing stress helps, and a doctor's review is worthwhile since it is a treatable cause of irregular periods.
    ▼If this reading is lowA lower prolactin reading outside of pregnancy or breastfeeding is reassuring, it means ovulation and cycles aren't being disrupted. Managing stress helps keep levels steady.
    Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH)
    Follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) is produced by the pituitary gland and is essential for female fertility. At the start of each menstrual cycle, FSH stimulates several follicles in the ovaries to begin developing — each follicle containing a potential egg. Usually, one follicle becomes dominant and produces increasing amounts of oestrogen as it matures. FSH also works together with LH to trigger ovulation and support normal menstrual cycles. FSH levels are routinely measured to assess ovarian reserve — high FSH indicates the pituitary is working harder to stimulate ageing or depleted ovaries, a sign of declining fertility or approaching menopause.
    ▲If this reading is highHigh FSH suggests the ovaries are working harder than usual, often linked to declining ovarian reserve or approaching menopause, so this is worth discussing with a doctor if fertility is a concern.
    ▼If this reading is lowA lower FSH reading is reassuring, it suggests the ovaries are not having to work especially hard, which usually points to a healthy ovarian reserve. Staying generally healthy supports this.

    Respiratory Function

    4 parameters
    Volume Tidal(VT)
    Tidal volume is the amount of air inhaled and exhaled with each normal, relaxed breath — typically about 500 mL in a healthy adult at rest. It naturally adjusts based on body size, metabolic demands, and activity level. During exercise, tidal volume increases to deliver more oxygen. Consistently low tidal volume at rest may indicate restrictive lung disease or respiratory muscle weakness.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher tidal volume isn't a concern in itself, it can simply reflect strong lung capacity or higher activity levels. Staying active and practicing good breathing habits helps keep your lungs working efficiently.
    ▼If this reading is lowConsistently low tidal volume may mean your lungs or breathing muscles are working under capacity, so practicing deep breathing exercises and staying active can help, with a doctor's check if it persists.
    Inspiratory Volume(TI)
    Inspiratory capacity is the maximum amount of air you can inhale starting from a normal relaxed breath out — about 3,000 mL in a healthy adult. It reflects how much extra breath reserve you have above your resting tidal volume. A reduced inspiratory volume can indicate lung disease, weakened breathing muscles, or restricted chest expansion.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher inspiratory volume is a good sign, it means you have plenty of breath reserve above your resting breathing. Staying active and practicing deep breathing helps maintain this capacity.
    ▼If this reading is lowA reduced inspiratory volume suggests less breath reserve than ideal, so breathing exercises and staying physically active can help, and a doctor's review is useful if breathlessness continues.
    Functional Residual Capacity(FRC)
    Functional residual capacity is the amount of air left in the lungs after a normal relaxed breath out — typically about 2,300–2,500 mL. This residual air keeps the airways open and allows gas exchange to continue between breaths. In emphysema, FRC increases as air becomes trapped in over-inflated lungs. In fibrosis, FRC decreases as stiff lungs deflate further than normal.
    ▲If this reading is highAn increased FRC can suggest air trapping in the lungs, so avoiding smoke exposure and discussing persistent breathlessness with a doctor is a good step.
    ▼If this reading is lowA decreased FRC can suggest stiffer lungs, so gentle breathing exercises and a doctor's review are worthwhile if you notice ongoing shortness of breath.
    Residual Volume(RV)
    Residual volume is the air that remains in the lungs even after you have exhaled as forcefully as possible — it cannot be breathed out. Typically about 1,200 mL, it prevents the lungs from collapsing completely. In emphysema, residual volume increases significantly as damaged airways trap air. Residual volume is measured indirectly (since it cannot be breathed into a spirometer) using gas dilution or body plethysmography.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher residual volume can suggest trapped air in the lungs, so avoiding smoke and irritants and mentioning persistent breathing changes to a doctor is sensible.
    ▼If this reading is lowA lower residual volume is reassuring, it suggests your lungs are emptying well without trapped air. Staying active and avoiding smoke exposure helps keep your lungs working efficiently.

    Lecithin

    6 parameters
    Phospholipid index
    Phospholipids are fat molecules that form the double-layered membrane (the bilayer) surrounding every cell in the body. This membrane controls what enters and exits the cell. Phospholipids also help transport fats through the bloodstream, prevent fatty liver, lower blood cholesterol, and improve circulation. Adequate phospholipids are essential for cell membrane health throughout the body.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading is a good sign, it means your cell membranes have plenty of building material and fat transport is working well. A balanced diet with foods like eggs and fish helps maintain this.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow phospholipid levels can affect cell membrane health and fat transport, so foods like eggs, soybeans, and fish can help support healthy levels.
    Sphingolipid index
    Sphingolipids are complex lipids that are key structural components of cell membranes — particularly in nerve cells and brain tissue. They are especially concentrated in the central nervous system, where they help form the myelin sheaths that insulate nerve fibres and speed up nerve signal transmission. Abnormal accumulation of sphingolipids in tissues causes serious neurological diseases (sphingolipidoses).
    ▲If this reading is highUnusually high accumulation in tissues is linked to rare neurological conditions and is worth a doctor's review if flagged.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow levels may affect nerve membrane health, so a balanced diet supporting overall nervous system health is a sensible everyday step.
    Sphingomyelin index
    Sphingomyelin is a type of sphingolipid that is a major building block of animal cell membranes. It plays a crucial role in maintaining cell membrane integrity and is particularly important in the nervous system, where it forms part of the myelin sheath around nerve fibres. Sphingomyelin levels affect cell signalling and are being studied in relation to cardiovascular disease and Alzheimer's disease.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading is reassuring, it suggests strong cell membrane integrity, including the nerve fibre coatings that depend on it. A diet with healthy fats helps keep this structural fat well supplied.
    ▼If this reading is lowLower levels may affect cell signalling and nerve membrane health, so a diet rich in healthy fats can help support this structural fat.
    Lecithin index
    Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) is a phospholipid that is sometimes called the 'third essential nutrient' alongside proteins and vitamins. It is a major component of cell membranes and is essential for brain function (as a precursor to the neurotransmitter acetylcholine), liver health (preventing fat accumulation), and fat metabolism. Good dietary sources include egg yolks, sunflower seeds, and soybeans.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading is a good sign, it means your brain and liver have plenty of this key building block to work with. Keeping foods like egg yolks, sunflower seeds, and soybeans in your diet helps maintain it.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow lecithin can affect brain and liver function, so adding egg yolks, sunflower seeds, or soybeans to your diet can help boost levels naturally.

    Brain phospholipids are natural, biologically active fats concentrated in brain cell membranes. They support the brain's unique functions — including cell-to-cell signalling, neurotransmitter release, and maintaining the fluidity and integrity of brain cell membranes. Adequate brain phospholipids are associated with sharper cognitive function and better mood regulation. They are non-toxic, non-irritating, and naturally derived.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading is reassuring, it suggests your brain cell membranes are well supplied for sharp thinking and steady mood. Omega-rich foods like fish and nuts help keep levels topped up.
    ▼If this reading is lowLower levels here may affect mental sharpness and mood, so omega-rich foods like fish and nuts can help support brain cell membrane health.

    Liposomes are microscopic spheres made from phospholipids (primarily lecithin) that have the same double-layer structure as cell membranes. In the body's own tissues, this type of structure is used to protect and transport lipid-soluble molecules. In skincare, liposomal formulations can penetrate deeply into skin tissue, delivering active ingredients directly into skin cells and providing excellent moisture retention.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading is a good sign, it means your body has a strong capacity to transport and protect lipid-soluble nutrients. A diet with healthy fats and good hydration helps maintain this naturally.
    ▼If this reading is lowA lower reading may mean reduced ability to transport and protect lipid-soluble nutrients, so a diet with healthy fats and good hydration supports this naturally.

    Fatty acid

    4 parameters
    Saturated fatty acid index
    Saturated fatty acids are fats where all carbon atoms are fully bonded to hydrogen atoms — making them solid at room temperature (like butter and lard). They are found mainly in animal products and tropical oils. A high saturated fat intake raises LDL (bad) cholesterol and is associated with increased cardiovascular disease risk. However, not all saturated fats are equally harmful — short and medium-chain ones behave differently from long-chain saturated fats.
    ▲If this reading is highHigh saturated fat intake raises LDL cholesterol, so cutting back on fatty meats and full-fat dairy in favour of leaner proteins can help lower this over time.
    ▼If this reading is lowA lower saturated fat reading is a good sign, it suggests your LDL cholesterol isn't being pushed up by this type of fat. Keeping fatty meats and full-fat dairy in moderation helps maintain this.
    Unsaturated fatty acid index
    Unsaturated fatty acids — both monounsaturated (olive oil, avocado) and polyunsaturated (fish oil, flaxseed) — are liquid at room temperature and are associated with health benefits. They help lower LDL cholesterol, reduce inflammation, support brain function, and maintain healthy cell membranes. Replacing saturated fats with unsaturated fats is consistently linked to reduced cardiovascular risk.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading here is a good thing, not a concern, it means you're getting plenty of the fats that support heart and brain health. Keep enjoying olive oil, avocado, nuts, and fish to maintain it.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow unsaturated fat intake means missing out on heart and brain benefits, so add more olive oil, avocado, nuts, and fish to your meals.
    Essential fatty acid index
    Essential fatty acids (omega-3 and omega-6) cannot be made by the body and must come from food. They are critical for brain development, immune function, hormone production, and controlling inflammation. Omega-3s (from oily fish, flaxseeds, walnuts) help reduce inflammation; omega-6s (from vegetable oils) promote it in excess. The balance between these two is important for overall health.
    ▲If this reading is highA higher reading is reassuring, it means your body has plenty of these fats to support brain, immune, and hormone function. Continuing to include oily fish, flaxseeds, and walnuts helps keep levels up.
    ▼If this reading is lowLow essential fatty acid levels can affect brain, immune, and hormone function, so oily fish, flaxseeds, and walnuts are good everyday sources to add.
    Triglyceride index
    Triglycerides are the most common fat molecules in the body — formed when glycerol combines with three fatty acids. They are the primary form of stored energy and circulate in the bloodstream. Elevated blood triglycerides are an independent risk factor for coronary heart disease and are closely associated with other metabolic risk factors, including low HDL cholesterol, high blood sugar, and abdominal obesity.
    ▲If this reading is highElevated triglycerides raise heart disease risk, so reducing sugar, alcohol, and refined carbs while staying active can help bring levels down.
    ▼If this reading is lowA lower triglyceride reading is a good sign, it means this risk factor for heart disease is well controlled. Staying active and keeping sugar and alcohol moderate helps maintain it.
